Built-in bookshelves are one of the most requested carpentry projects we get, and one of the hardest to price from a photo. A simple painted bookcase on one wall and a fireplace surround with cabinets, lights and crown are both "built-ins", and they are very different jobs. This guide explains what built-ins cost, what drives the price and what separates built-ins that look like part of the house from ones that look like furniture pushed against a wall.

## What built-in bookshelves cost

Custom built-ins are usually priced per linear foot of wall. National cost guides put them at roughly **$150 to $600 per linear foot** for typical painted work, with fancy hardwood and complex designs above **$1,000 per foot**. Bob Vila reports a range of about $150 to $1,200 or more per linear foot for custom work, and a national average around $2,850 for a project ([Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/built-in-bookshelves-cost/)). Fireplace surrounds with cabinets below commonly land in the middle to upper part of that range.

These are planning numbers. The real price depends on the design, which is why we always price from a sketch after measuring.

## What drives the price

### Size

Height and width are the obvious factors. A floor-to-ceiling unit in a room with nine-foot ceilings needs a rolling ladder for access and has more material per foot than a waist-high unit.

### Open shelves vs cabinets

Open shelves are the least expensive. Cabinets with doors below add boxes, doors, hinges and hardware. Drawers add more. Many people choose cabinets below and open shelves above, which hides clutter and gives a counter surface.

### Material

- **Paint-grade plywood boxes with poplar face frames:** the standard for good painted built-ins. Strong, stable and paints well.
- **MDF:** smooth and cheap, good for panels and doors, but heavy, weak at screws and prone to sagging as long shelves.
- **Stain-grade hardwood** (oak, maple, walnut, cherry): costs more in material and much more in labor, since every joint shows.

### Details

Crown molding, base, fluted columns, beadboard backs, lighting, glass doors and adjustable shelves each add time. Lighting also adds an electrician.

### The room

Walls that are not plumb, floors that are not level and ceilings that slope are normal, especially in older houses. Built-ins have to be scribed to them, which takes time. Built-ins under sloped ceilings or in knee walls, like the ones we built in Lower Merion, need careful layout.

## Shelf sag: the detail that shows quality

Long shelves loaded with books sag over time. How much depends on the material, thickness, span and load. Ways to prevent it:

- **Keep spans reasonable.** For books, about 30 to 36 inches between supports for a 3/4-inch plywood shelf.
- **Add a solid wood nosing** on the front edge, which stiffens the shelf and hides the plywood edge.
- **Use fixed shelves** at intervals to stiffen the case.
- **Avoid long MDF shelves.** MDF sags more than plywood.

Sagging shelves are the most common complaint about cheap built-ins, and the easiest to avoid at the design stage.

## How built-ins are made to look built in

The difference between a built-in and a bookcase standing against the wall is in the fit:

1. **Scribed to the wall and floor.** The sides are cut to follow the wall, so there is no gap.
2. **Tied into the room trim.** The room's baseboard runs into the base of the built-in; the crown continues across the top.
3. **Face frames line up** with nearby window and door casings.
4. **Painted with the trim,** so it reads as part of the house.
5. **No visible fasteners.** Holes filled, joints caulked, edges sanded.

## Common types of built-ins

- **Fireplace surrounds:** bookcases on both sides of a fireplace, often with cabinets below and a mantel connecting them.
- **Window seats between bookcases:** a seat with storage under a window, flanked by shelves.
- **Built-in desks:** a desk top in a nook with shelves above. We built two in paneled alcoves in a Lower Merion house.
- **Closet shelving:** solid shelving and rods built to the closet instead of wire.
- **Knee-wall storage:** shelves or cabinets set into the low walls of attic rooms.
- **Mudroom lockers:** cubbies with hooks, a bench and shelves.
- **Pantry shelving.**

## Built on site or in a shop?

There are two ways to make built-ins:

- **Shop-built:** boxes and doors are made in a cabinet shop, finished, and installed. Best for complex cabinetry and sprayed finishes.
- **Site-built:** cases are built from sheet goods, fitted in place and painted on site. Best for painted built-ins that need to fit an unusual space.

We mostly build on site, which suits painted work in older houses where nothing is square.

## How long it takes

A single-wall built-in usually takes two to five days on site, plus painting. A fireplace surround with cabinets, or a closet with multiple walls, takes longer. If cabinet doors or glass are ordered, add lead time.

## Do built-ins add value?

Well-made built-ins that match the house are usually seen by buyers as a plus, especially in older houses where they fit the character. Poorly made ones, sagging shelves, peeling paint or units that look like furniture, can be seen as something to remove.

## How to prepare for a quote

Bring us:

- A photo or two of what you like.
- Rough dimensions of the wall.
- What will go on the shelves: books, TV, display items. This decides depth and spacing.
- Whether you want doors, drawers, lighting or outlets.

We will measure, sketch the built-in with shelf spacing and trim, and price it.

## Bottom line

- Expect roughly $150 to $600 per linear foot for typical painted built-ins, more for hardwood and complex designs.
- Cabinets, drawers, lighting and crown add cost; open shelves cost least.
- Plywood boxes and solid edges keep shelves from sagging.
- Scribing and matching trim is what makes built-ins look built in.

## A sample layout: fireplace wall

Here is how we typically lay out bookcases on both sides of a fireplace, the most common built-in request:

1. **Measure the alcoves** on each side of the chimney breast at the floor, the middle and the top, since they are rarely equal or square.
2. **Set the cabinet height** a little below the mantel, often 30 to 36 inches, so the cabinet tops read as a ledge.
3. **Divide the shelves above** into even spaces, with the top shelf lining up with something in the room, such as the window head.
4. **Match the face frames** to the width of the window and door casings nearby.
5. **Carry the room's crown** across the top of each bookcase, or stop the bookcases just below the ceiling with their own cap.
6. **Plan outlets and lights** before building, especially if a TV will sit above the mantel.

The result looks like it was built with the house.

## Questions people ask about built-ins

**Can built-ins be removed later?** Yes. Built-ins are screwed to the wall framing, not glued to it, and can be taken out with some patching of drywall and flooring. Planning outlets and lighting so they do not depend on the built-in makes removal easier.

**Should the back be painted the same color as the shelves?** It is a style choice. A darker or contrasting back makes books and objects stand out; matching the shelves makes the unit feel lighter. Beadboard backs add texture.

**How deep should bookshelves be?** For most books, 10 to 12 inches of depth is enough. Twelve to 14 inches suits large art books and display pieces. Cabinets below are usually 15 to 24 inches deep, which also creates a ledge at the top of the base.

**How far apart should shelves be?** Around 10 to 12 inches for standard books, 13 to 15 inches for larger books and objects. Adjustable shelves let you change it later.

**Can you build around a TV?** Yes. Plan the TV size, the wiring path and ventilation for any equipment in cabinets before building. TVs change faster than built-ins, so leave some extra width.

**Can built-ins go on an exterior wall?** Yes, with care for insulation and condensation in older houses. We leave a small air space or insulate behind the back where the wall is cold.

## Lighting options

- **Puck or strip LED lights** under each shelf or in the top of the unit.
- **Picture lights** mounted above the unit.
- **Sconces** on the face frame.

Lighting needs an electrician for hardwired fixtures. Low-voltage LED strips can sometimes plug in, but hardwired with a wall switch is cleaner.

## Planning tips that save money

1. Keep the design to one wall where possible; corners and returns add time.
2. Use open shelves above and a simple cabinet base below.
3. Choose painted rather than stained finishes.
4. Standardize shelf spacing and cabinet sizes.
5. Combine built-ins with other trim work in the same room, so setup and painting happen once.
