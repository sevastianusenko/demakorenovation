If your exterior door frame and sill are solid and square, replacing only the door slab can cost a few hundred dollars less than a full replacement. If the sill is soft, the jamb is rotted at the bottom, or the door has never closed right, you need the door and the frame, which usually means a new prehung unit at about $1,000 to $3,500 installed in 2026. Most exterior doors we look at need the frame. Here is how to check yours.

## The short version

| Situation | What you need | Typical cost in 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Door is damaged or ugly, frame and sill are solid and square | New slab in the old frame | $400 to $1,500 plus the door |
| Frame is solid, sill is worn but not rotted | New threshold or sill cap, maybe new slab | $200 to $600 for the sill work |
| Sill or bottom of the jambs is soft | New prehung door and frame | $1,000 to $3,500 |
| Rot reaches the subfloor or rim joist | New prehung door plus floor framing repair | $1,500 to $5,000+ |
| Door has sidelights and the unit is failing | New entry system | $2,500 to $7,000+ |

These ranges come from 2026 national cost guides. Repairs to floor framing vary the most, because nobody knows how far rot has gone until the old sill is out.

## What the frame actually does

An exterior door frame is three parts: two side jambs, a head jamb, and a sill or threshold at the bottom. It holds the hinges, the strike, the weatherstripping and the seal at the bottom. If any of those parts is out of square, worn or rotted, a new door hung in it will stick, leak or let in drafts.

That is why we treat the frame as the real question, not the door.

## Six checks you can do yourself

1. **Push a screwdriver into the sill**, especially at the corners and the outside edge. Soft wood means rot.
2. **Check the bottom of the jambs**, the first 6 inches above the sill, inside and out. That is where water collects.
3. **Look at the floor inside the door.** A soft spot, stained flooring or a dip means water has been getting under the sill.
4. **Look at the gap around the closed door.** It should be even on the sides and top. A wedge-shaped gap means the frame is out of square or the house has settled.
5. **Check the hinge side.** Loose hinge screws that will not tighten mean the jamb wood is stripped or soft.
6. **Watch the door in a storm.** Water on the floor or daylight under the door means the sill seal or flashing has failed.

If you find one or more of these, plan on a new frame.

## When a slab replacement is fine

Replacing only the door slab is a good choice when:

- The frame and sill are solid and the door closes evenly.
- You are keeping trim you like, such as an old wood casing on a historic house.
- The door itself is the problem: dented steel, cracked wood panels or a door style you want to change.

A slab has to be fitted to the old frame: hinge locations, lock bore height and the size of the opening all have to match. Old frames are rarely perfect, so expect some fitting. It is still often the right choice for a sound frame.

## When you need the door and frame

You need a new prehung door and frame when:

- The sill or jambs are rotted.
- The frame is far out of square.
- You want to change the door size, add sidelights or change the swing.
- You want a modern sill with a proper seal and drainage.

A prehung unit comes with a new frame, sill, weatherstripping and hinges already set. Installing it properly includes a sill pan or flashing under the sill, so water that gets past the threshold drains out instead of into the floor.

## What happens under the sill

This is where the price changes. When we remove an old door, we look at the subfloor and rim joist under the sill. In many older houses we find:

- Rotted subfloor under the threshold.
- A soft rim joist where water has run down for years.
- Bottom plates of the wall framing beside the door gone soft.

All of that has to be replaced before the new door goes in. It is a carpentry repair, not a door repair, and it is why we include a unit price for sill and floor repair in every door estimate. You see it and approve it before we go ahead.

## How a full door and frame replacement goes

1. Protect the floor inside and set up a temporary barrier if the weather is bad.
2. Remove the old door, casing, frame and sill.
3. Inspect the subfloor, rim joist and jamb studs, and repair any rot.
4. Install a sloped sill pan or flashing under the sill.
5. Set the new prehung unit, shim it plumb and square at the hinges and strike, and fasten it.
6. Check the reveal and adjust until the door closes and seals evenly.
7. Insulate the gap and flash the sides and head.
8. Install exterior trim or brickmold, interior casing and hardware.
9. Caulk, clean up and test the lock and seal.

Most standard doors take a day. Doors with sidelights or floor repairs can take two.

## Choosing the new unit

If you are replacing the frame, you can choose a better unit:

- **Fiberglass** for a front door you want to last with little upkeep.
- **Steel** for a solid, lower-cost back or side door.
- **Wood** for historic houses and covered entries.
- **Composite frames and sills**, which do not rot, are worth asking about on doors that get weather.

## Permits and contracts in Pennsylvania

Replacing a door and frame in the same opening does not usually need a building permit under Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code. Widening an opening, cutting a new header or changing an exit door may. Home improvement contracts over $500 must be in writing, with a three business day right to cancel.

## Thresholds and weatherstripping

Sometimes a door that leaks air or water does not need a new frame at all, just new seals. Before you replace anything, check:

- **The door sweep** at the bottom of the door. These wear out and are cheap to replace.
- **The threshold height.** Many thresholds have adjustment screws that raise the cap to meet the sweep.
- **The weatherstripping** in the jambs. Compression strips flatten over the years and can usually be pulled out and replaced.
- **The strike position.** If the door does not pull tight against the weatherstripping when locked, moving the strike a little can close the gap.

If those are fine and the door still leaks, the problem is in the frame, the sill or the flashing, and that is when replacement makes sense.

## Questions people ask about door and frame replacement

**Can a rotted door frame be repaired instead of replaced?** Sometimes. If only the bottom few inches of a jamb are soft, a carpenter can cut out the bad section and splice in new wood or PVC. If the sill is also bad, replacement is usually better value.

**How much does it cost to replace a door frame only?** Replacing a frame without a new door is rare, because the old door rarely fits a new frame well. Most frame replacements are done with a new prehung door.

**Why does my new door leak at the bottom?** Usually because there is no sill pan or flashing under it, or the sill is not supported and has flexed. Both are installation issues.

**Can you keep my old interior trim?** Often, yes. We remove it carefully and reinstall it if it is in good shape.

**Is the estimate free?** Yes. We check the sill, jambs and floor, and give you a written estimate with any repair priced separately.
