Seamless siding is steel siding that is formed on site, from a coil, into pieces as long as each wall, so there are no overlapping joints along the run. It usually costs about $6 to $14 per square foot installed in 2026, roughly one and a half to two times the price of vinyl lap siding and close to fiber cement. It looks clean and lasts a long time. It is also harder to repair and match later, and fewer contractors install it. Here is how it compares, so you can decide if it is right for your house.

## What "seamless" means

Normal lap siding, whether vinyl, fiber cement or wood, comes in boards about 12 feet long. On a long wall, the boards overlap or butt together at joints, which you can see as lines across the wall. Installers stagger them so they look less obvious.

Seamless steel siding is made by feeding a coil of pre-finished steel through a machine on a truck at your house. The machine forms each piece to the exact length of the wall, so a 40 foot wall gets 40 foot pieces. The only joints are at corners, windows and doors.

## Advantages of seamless steel siding

- **No visible joints** along the wall, which gives a clean, even look.
- **Fewer places for water to get behind the siding** along the run.
- **Strong and fire resistant.** Steel does not burn and resists hail better than vinyl.
- **Pre-finished color** that does not need painting for many years.
- **Does not rot or attract insects.**

## Disadvantages

- **Repairs are harder.** A dent or damaged piece on a long wall may mean replacing a full-length piece, which needs the installer to come back with the machine and matching coil.
- **Color matching over time.** The factory finish fades slowly. A new piece years later may not match the old color exactly.
- **Dents.** Steel resists hail better than vinyl but can still dent from a ladder, a ball or a lawn mower.
- **Fewer installers.** Because it needs special equipment, there are fewer companies to compare and fewer to call for repairs years from now.
- **Rust at cut edges** if the coating is damaged and not touched up.
- **Long pieces need careful handling** on site, and the house needs room for the truck and long pieces.
- **Thermal movement.** Long steel pieces expand and contract with temperature and need to be fastened to allow it.

## Lap siding options compared

### Vinyl

The lowest cost and the most common. It has visible joints on long walls, can look thin on older houses, and can warp near heat. It is easy to repair, because damaged pieces can be unlocked and replaced.

### Fiber cement

Our most common recommendation for houses people plan to keep. It does not rot or burn, holds paint for many years and looks like painted wood. Joints are visible but are flashed behind and can be placed and staggered carefully. Repairs are straightforward, since boards can be replaced one at a time and repainted. Our [fiber cement siding cost guide](/blog/fiber-cement-siding-cost/) covers it in detail.

### Engineered wood

LP SmartSide comes in longer boards than most lap siding, often 16 feet, which means fewer joints. It looks like wood at a lower cost than fiber cement. Cut edges must be sealed.

### Cedar

The traditional choice on older houses, with the most maintenance. On the stone house in Lower Merion we used lap siding on the walls and cedar shakes on the gambrel ends.

## Joints are not the enemy

The main selling point of seamless siding is no joints. But on well-installed lap siding, joints are not a weak point. In fiber cement, each butt joint has a flashing strip behind it, so water that gets into the joint drains out over the housewrap. In vinyl, joints overlap and the siding hangs loose so it can move. With longer boards and careful layout, joints can be placed where they are least visible, such as near windows.

What matters most for keeping water out of a wall is the layer behind the siding: housewrap or a sheathing system like ZIP System, and flashing at every window, door and roof-to-wall joint. Our guide on [ZIP System versus housewrap](/blog/zip-system-vs-housewrap/) explains that layer. Any siding, seamless or lap, over a badly flashed wall will let water in.

## How to decide

Seamless steel siding can be a good choice if:

- You want a very clean, joint-free look on long, simple walls.
- You live where hail or fire is a real concern.
- There is a reputable local installer who will be around for repairs.

Lap siding is usually the better choice if:

- The house has many windows, corners and short walls, where seamless has less advantage.
- You want the look of painted wood or cedar, especially on an older house.
- You want simple, piece-by-piece repairs in the future.
- You want the widest choice of installers and materials.

## Questions to ask any siding contractor

- What goes behind the siding: housewrap or ZIP System, and how are windows flashed?
- What happens if the sheathing is rotted once the old siding is off?
- How are joints handled, and where will they fall?
- Who repairs a damaged piece in ten years, and can the color be matched?
- What trim is included around windows, doors and corners?
- What warranty covers the material, and what covers the installation?

## Removing old siding first

Whatever you choose, we recommend removing old siding rather than covering it. It is the only way to see and fix the sheathing, flash the windows and keep the trim from looking sunken. Our guide on [removing old siding before new siding](/blog/remove-old-siding-before-new-siding/) explains why.

## Insulated siding

Some homeowners compare seamless steel with insulated vinyl, which has foam bonded to the back of each panel. The foam makes the siding stiffer and straighter on uneven walls and adds a little insulation. Fiber cement and steel can also go over a layer of rigid foam on the wall, which does more for energy than foam on the siding itself.

## Permits and contracts in Pennsylvania

Re-siding usually does not require a building permit in Lancaster County townships, though some do and historic districts may require review. Structural repairs and new openings are more likely to need one. Home improvement contracts over $500 must be in writing in Pennsylvania, with three business days to cancel, and the deposit on jobs over $1,000 is limited to one third of the price plus special-order materials. Houses built before 1978 need lead-safe practices when old painted siding comes off.

## Questions people ask about seamless siding

**Is seamless siding better than vinyl?** It is stronger, more fire resistant and has no joints along the wall. Vinyl costs less and is easier to repair. Which is better depends on the house, the budget and local installers.

**How long does seamless steel siding last?** Steel siding with a good factory finish commonly lasts 40 years or more, with the finish fading slowly over time.

**Can seamless siding be repaired?** Yes, but damaged pieces may need to be replaced full length by an installer with the forming machine, and the color may not match exactly after many years.

**Is seamless siding the same as seamless gutters?** No. Seamless gutters are formed on site the same way, from a coil, but they are a separate product. Many houses have seamless gutters with lap siding.
